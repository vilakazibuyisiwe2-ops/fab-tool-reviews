Take a look at that photo. A guy grinding steel, sparks flying everywhere — and he's in sandals, bare feet right next to the spark shower, with no gloves on the hand holding the work steady. Honestly? It is exactly the kind of shortcut that turns a routine job into an injury.

## What could actually go wrong

- **Bare feet in sandals:** Sparks cool as they fly, but they are still hot when they land. One lands on bare skin, you flinch, and now you have moved the grinder mid-cut.
- **No gloves:** If the disc catches or the metal shifts, your hand is right there with nothing between it and trouble. Gloves do not make a grinder safe, but suitable gloves can provide useful protection from heat and sharp edges.
- **The guard:** The guard redirects sparks and helps contain fragments if a disc shatters. Keep it fitted, secure, and correctly positioned.
- **Posture and grip:** Keep solid footing and use both hands where the tool design requires it. If the disc binds and kicks, your stance matters.

## Don't do it like I did

I once cut clean through a power cord while grinding in a workshop. I did not see it — it was just in the disc's path — and then there was a loud, explosive bang.

Learn from that one: **tuck cables away, and keep the grinder's own cable behind you, never crossing in front of where you are cutting.**

## Quick checklist before you switch it on

- Cables tucked away and the grinder cord routed behind you
- Workspace clear of clutter and anything else in the spark zone
- Disc checked for cracks and mounted tight and correctly
- Guard fixed firmly in place
- Good stance and steady grip
- Clear safety glasses on
- No flammables anywhere near the spark trail
- A barrier up if anyone else is working nearby

None of this takes long. The few seconds spent checking are worth far more than the consequences of a shortcut.
