## Measuring and Layout Tools

### Calipers
- **Vernier calipers:** Measure internal/external dimensions to 0.05 mm
- **Digital calipers:** Easier to read, battery-powered
- **Dial calipers:** Mechanical, no battery required

### Rulers and Scales
- **Steel ruler (12–24 inches):** For straight lines and measurements
- **Try square:** Verify right angles
- **Combination square:** Adjustable for 45° and 90° angles
- **Layout fluid and scribe:** Mark cutting and drilling points

## Cutting Tools

### Hacksaws
- 24–32 teeth per inch for general metal
- 32 teeth per inch for stainless and harder materials
- Keep blades sharp; replace when teeth dull

### Angle Grinders
- Essential for cutting, grinding, and polishing
- Always use a face shield and cut-resistant gloves
- Secure the work with a clamp

### Chisels and Punches
- **Cold chisel:** Cutting or shaping metal
- **Centre punch:** Mark drilling points
- **Prick punch:** Fine lines for layout

## Clamping and Holding

### Vises
- **Bench vise:** 4–5 inch jaws for general work
- **Locking pliers (Vise-Grips):** Portable, adjustable grip
- **C-clamps:** Quick-release for temporary holding

### Magnetic Holders
- **Welding magnets:** Hold pieces at precise angles
- **Magnetic squares:** Ensure 90° alignment

## Filing and Finishing

### Files
- **Bastard cut:** Coarse, removes material quickly
- **Second cut:** Medium, general smoothing
- **Smooth cut:** Fine finish
- Always use a handle; never file with bare hands

### Deburring Tools
- **Hand deburrer:** Smooth sharp edges post-cutting
- **Spiral flute deburrer:** Quick edge cleanup

## Safety Reminders

- Wear cut-resistant gloves when handling sharp edges
- Always secure your work before cutting or grinding
- Keep tools sharp—dull tools require more force and slip easily
- Inspect tools for damage before use
- Clean tools regularly to prevent rust
