## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Proper PPE is non-negotiable in the workshop. Every welding job requires:

- **Welding helmet** with proper shade (typically #10–#12)
- **Safety glasses** underneath (ANSI Z87.1 rated)
- **Flame-resistant apron** or leather jacket
- **Welding gloves** (leather or heat-resistant material)
- **Steel-toed boots** with ankle support
- **Long sleeves** (cotton or flame-resistant fabric)

## Ventilation and Fume Extraction

Welding produces harmful fumes. Always:

- Work in a well-ventilated area or use a fume extractor
- Position the extraction nozzle 6–12 inches from the arc
- Check local regulations on fume exposure limits
- Avoid welding in confined spaces without proper ventilation

## Pre-Work Equipment Checks

Before each shift, inspect:

- **Cables and connectors** for cuts or damage
- **Ground clamp** for secure connection
- **Helmet lens** for cracks
- **Work area** for trip hazards or fire risks
- **Gas cylinders** for proper valve closure and secure placement

## Fire Prevention

- Keep a fire extinguisher rated for metal fires within arm's reach
- Clear the work area of flammable materials (cloth, wood, solvents)
- Notify others when you're performing hot work
- Allow materials to cool before touching or moving them

## Emergency Response

- Know the location of first aid kits and eyewash stations
- Never weld alone; have a buddy system in place
- In case of eye flash, use cool water or eyewash—seek medical attention if pain persists
