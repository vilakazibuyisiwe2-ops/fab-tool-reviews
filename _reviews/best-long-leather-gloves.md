Your welding helmet protects your eyes, face and neck from arc flash, sparks and spatter. It is the one piece of kit you should never cheap out on. Here is what actually matters when you shop.

## 1. Shade range

Auto-darkening helmets switch from a light shade to a dark shade the moment you strike an arc.

- **Variable shade 9-13** covers most MIG, stick and general fabrication.
- **Variable shade 5-13** is better if you do low-amp TIG or plasma work.
- A helmet that also has a **grind mode** (light shade, no darkening) saves you swapping helmets.

## 2. Optical clarity rating

Look for the rating written as four numbers, for example **1/1/1/1**. The lower the numbers, the clearer and more accurate your view. A 1/1/1/1 lens reduces eye strain over a long day.

## 3. Arc sensors

More sensors mean fewer moments where the lens fails to darken because a sensor was blocked. **Four sensors** is the sweet spot for pipe work, awkward positions and out-of-position welding. Two sensors is fine for simple flat work.

## 4. Reaction time

This is how fast the lens darkens. Faster is safer for your eyes. Look for **1/20,000 second or faster**.

## 5. Power and weight

- **Solar-assisted with replaceable batteries** is the most reliable.
- Choose a helmet you can wear comfortably for hours. A heavy, badly balanced helmet strains your neck, and a sore neck ends your workday early.
- Check the headgear has good adjustment (tilt, distance and tightness).

## 6. Safety standards

Only buy helmets that state compliance with a recognised standard, such as **ANSI Z87.1** or **EN 379 / EN 175**. If a listing does not mention any standard, skip it.

## Quick buying checklist

- Shade range that fits your process (MIG/stick, TIG or all)
- Optical clarity 1/1/1/1
- Four arc sensors
- Fast reaction time
- Grind mode
- Certified to a safety standard
- Replacement cover lenses easy to find

## Care tips

- Replace the outer cover lens when it is scratched or pitted by spatter.
- Test the auto-darkening function before every job by striking an arc.
- Store the helmet away from heat and direct sun.
- Wear safety glasses **under** your helmet.

## Final word

A good helmet is an investment in your eyesight. Match the shade range to your process, insist on good optical clarity, and buy from a brand that sells spare lenses.
