Angle grinders are one of the most common tools in a fabrication workshop, but they are also one of the easiest tools to use unsafely.

A grinder can cut metal quickly, remove rust, shape edges, and clean welds — but if the tool is used incorrectly, it can cause serious injuries in seconds.

## Before you switch it on

Start with the basics:

- Check the disc for cracks, chips, or damage.
- Make sure the correct disc is fitted for the material and task.
- Confirm the guard is in place and adjusted correctly.
- Inspect the power cord, handle, and trigger.
- Secure the workpiece so it cannot move unexpectedly.

Never use a damaged grinding or cutting disc. If a disc is worn out or cracked, replace it before use.

## Wear the correct PPE

Use proper personal protective equipment every time:

- Safety glasses or a face shield
- Hearing protection
- Heavy-duty gloves where appropriate
- Safety boots
- Flame-resistant clothing if you are working around sparks
- Respiratory protection when required by the work area

Sparks, metal fragments, and abrasive dust are all normal hazards when grinding. Good PPE is not optional.

## Safe setup and handling

- Hold the grinder with both hands when possible.
- Keep the guard between you and the cutting surface.
- Do not force the tool into the workpiece.
- Let the disc do the work.
- Keep your body clear of the line of fire.
- Keep flammable materials and gases away from the work area.

Avoid using the side of a cutting disc for grinding. Discs are designed for a specific job, and using them the wrong way can cause failure.

## Watch your position

Standing in the wrong place can turn a minor task into a dangerous one. Make sure you have a stable footing and a clear path away from the machine.

Your hands should be in a comfortable, controlled position, with no chance of slipping into the disc. If the workpiece is small, clamp it down before you begin.

## After use

Switch off the grinder and allow it to stop before putting it down. Disconnect power before changing discs or carrying out maintenance.

Clean the tool, inspect it again, and store it safely in a dry, clean place.

## A simple rule to remember

If you are unsure whether the tool is suitable for the job, stop and check. A few seconds of inspection can prevent a serious injury.

> This article is for general educational purposes. Always follow the tool manufacturer's instructions, workplace procedures, and local safety requirements. If you are operating in a workplace, get the correct safety approval before use.
