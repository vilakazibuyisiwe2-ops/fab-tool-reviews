I still remember my first week as a boilermaker learner.

The fumes from the welding hit me hard — I hadn't gotten used to them yet. My hand was shaky. I was trying to hold the torch steady but I was completely out of balance. And my feet? My feet were burning.

The company boots they gave me were stiff, hot, and after 8 hours on concrete, every step was pain. I couldn't concentrate on the weld because all I could think about was my feet.

That's when I decided to invest in my own boots. And in insoles.

Best decision I ever made in the workshop.

### Why Cheap Company Boots Cost You More

When your feet hurt, you don't weld well. You shift your weight, you lose balance, your hand shakes even more. It's not just discomfort — it's a safety risk.

A good pair of boilermaker boots should do 3 things:

**1. Heat Resistance**
You're standing near slag, sparks, and hot plate. The sole must be heat-resistant rubber, not cheap PVC that melts.

**2. Ankle Support**
You are climbing, crouching, carrying plate. If your ankle rolls, you go down with a grinder in your hand. Look for high-cut, laced properly.

**3. Comfort for 10 Hours**
This is where insoles changed everything for me. Even a good boot is hard inside. A R150 memory foam insole made life much simpler. Suddenly I could concentrate on the bead, not the pain.

### What I Look For Now

After burning through 2 pairs, here's my cheat sheet:

- **Steel or composite toe** — both okay, composite is lighter for long days
- **Anti-penetration midsole** — you WILL step on wire and offcuts
- **Breathable lining** — your feet sweat, and sweaty feet blister
- **Replaceable insole** — this is non-negotiable for me now
